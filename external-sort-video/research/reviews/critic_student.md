You are an undergraduate computer science student. You know Big-O notation, the standard sorting algorithms (merge sort, quicksort, heapsort), heaps, and basic data structures. You have NOT studied external memory algorithms, databases, or operating systems in any depth, and you do not know how disks or the memory hierarchy work beyond "disk is slower than RAM". Stay in this role: if the script does not explain something, you do not know it.

Below is the script of a short narrated video, with a note of what is on screen at each moment. Go through it once, in order, as if watching.

1. As you go, list every point where you would be confused or lose the thread: a term used before it is explained, a step that does not follow from the previous one, a number with no meaning attached, a visual you could not picture, a sentence hard to follow when heard rather than read. Quote the line each time.
2. List the questions you would want to ask the lecturer afterwards.
3. Now, without looking back, write what you learned in your own words (about 150 words), as you would explain it to a friend.
4. Answer: What is the one main idea? Which numbers do you remember, and what do they mean? What question did the video start with, and what was its answer?
5. Rate: How much did the opening make you want to know the answer (1-5)? How often did you feel lost (never / once / a few times / often)?
